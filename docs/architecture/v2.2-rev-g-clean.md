# AudioPicture Rev.G — clean sheet

Status: **ARCHITECTURE_AUTHORITY / NOT_AN_ACOUSTIC_MEASUREMENT**

One picture, 320 × 400 × **30 mm**. Bought brain, one exciter, one printed frame. Rev.A through Rev.F are evidence, not the product.

## 1. Decision

The expensive path was a custom MAIN, PoE, Ethernet, a second Class-D, and a pair of OEM exciters. Under 30 mm that path does not buy better sound per euro. The Satellite1.1 Dev Kit already has the parts that take longest to get right: ESP32-S3, XMOS voice, four microphones, hardware mute, ESPHome, Snapcast, temperature, humidity, light, and a connector for an **LD2450** presence sensor.

So the product is:

| Function | Part | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brain, voice, mics, sensors, music | Satellite1.1 Dev Kit, $70 | Already works with Home Assistant |
| Presence | LD2450 on the kit connector, $10 | Latest HAT has the socket. 24 GHz, tracks up to 3 people |
| Power | USB-C PD 30 W, in the kit box | One conversion. No PoE magnetics |
| Radiator | Light panel, 6 mm, about 296 × 310 mm | The cone will not fit. The panel is the speaker |
| Motor | **One Visaton EX 60 S – 4** | 4 Ω matches the kit amp, Fs about 30 Hz, 21 mm deep, ~25 W |
| Amplifier | TAS2780 already on the HAT | 25 W mono. No second board, no extra loss |
| Network | Wi-Fi of the kit | Multi-room is Snapcast |
| Body | Printed frame, this repo | `mechanical/rev-g/cad/` |

Withdrawn, on purpose: Ethernet, PoE, 60 GHz radar, XVF3800, TAS5825M, dual exciters, custom ESP-IDF, Dayton DAEX32SQ-8.

## 2. Why this exciter

The HAT amplifier is one channel into **4 Ω**. The Visaton EX 60 S – 4 is that load, it is 21 mm deep, and its free-air resonance is about 30 Hz, so it is a panel woofer rather than a midrange buzzer. A DAEX32SQ-8 is 8 Ω, 130 g, and resonates at 481 Hz. A second amplifier would need an I2S tap we have not proved, another PCB, and another heat source, for a crossover we cannot tune until a panel exists.

Depth check, front of the cloth to back of the frame:

| Layer | mm |
| --- | ---: |
| Cloth | 1.5 |
| Panel | 6.0 |
| Exciter behind the panel | 21.0 |
| Air to the rear skin | 0.5 |
| Rear skin | 1.0 |
| **Total** | **30.0** |

The electronics do not sit behind the exciter. They sit in a bottom bay, where the boards are only 10 mm tall.

Honest acoustic bar, until a measurement says otherwise: wide, even sound, usable bass from roughly **60–80 Hz**, not a 50 Hz ±3 dB claim. One exciter on a light panel can be good. It is not an Amina twin-driver system, and it does not need to be for this cost.

## 3. Frame

Printed in PETG or ASA, front face on the bed. Files: `mechanical/rev-g/cad/AP-G-FRAME.stl` and `.step`.

- Outer 320 × 400 × 30 mm.
- Panel drops in behind the front lip. Cloth wraps the lip.
- Bottom bay holds the HAT (88 × 88 × 10) and the Core (66 × 31 × 10), side by side.
- USB-C enters through the bottom edge.
- LD2450 looks into the room through a plastic window in the bay. No metal over the antenna.
- Four microphone slots in the bay lip. Align them to the HAT once the board is in hand.
- French cleat on the rear. Design load 3 kg × 3. The kit, exciter, and frame are well under the old 5 kg cone budget.

## 5. Bring-up

1. Print the frame. Confirm the HAT, Core, and USB plug sit in the bay without standing proud of 30 mm.
2. Stick the exciter on the panel, off the geometric centre (seed in `rev_g_params.json`). Play from the kit amp before the cloth goes on.
3. Add cloth. Listen for buzz against the lip.
4. Fit LD2450, point it into the room, tune zones in the Sat1 UI.
5. Only if the bass is obviously short, change the panel material. Do not add a second exciter: two 4 Ω motors in parallel are 2 Ω, and the TAS2780 is not specified for that.

## 6. Multi-room

Two pictures are two Satellite1 units in one Snapcast group. Stereo is left and right assignment in Music Assistant, not a custom clock. That is the fast path, and it is already shipping in the kit firmware.
