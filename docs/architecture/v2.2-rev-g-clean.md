# AudioPicture Rev.G — clean sheet

Status: **ARCHITECTURE_AUTHORITY / NOT_AN_ACOUSTIC_MEASUREMENT**

One picture, 320 × 400 × **30 mm**. Satellite1.1 is the brain. The loudspeaker is the two-exciter panel from the earlier recommendation. Rev.A through Rev.F are evidence, not the product.

## 1. Decision

The expensive path was a custom MAIN, PoE, Ethernet and a 60 GHz radar. The Satellite1.1 Dev Kit already has ESP32-S3, XMOS voice, four microphones, hardware mute, ESPHome, Snapcast, temperature, humidity, light, and a connector for an **LD2450**. The acoustic parts stay the ones chosen for a 30 mm panel: a light diaphragm, a thin woofer exciter, a Tectonic wideband exciter, and a TAS5825M to drive them separately. The kit’s own TAS2780 is mono and is left unused.

So the product is:

| Function | Part | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brain, voice, mics, sensors, music | Satellite1.1 Dev Kit, $70 | Already works with Home Assistant |
| Presence | LD2450 on the kit connector, $10 | Latest HAT has the socket. 24 GHz, tracks up to 3 people |
| Power | USB-C PD **45 W or 65 W**, 20 V | Feeds the kit and the TAS5825M. No PoE |
| Radiator | Honeycomb panel, 8 mm, about 296 × 310 mm | The cone will not fit. The panel is the speaker |
| Bass motor | Thin woofer exciter, ≤ 16 mm, ~4 Ω | Billionsound RMD-class or an OEM LowFreq unit. Not a DAEX32 |
| Mid/high motor | Tectonic **TEAX25C10-8/HS** | 19 mm, about 85 g, roughly 100 Hz–20 kHz |
| Amplifier | **TAS5825M**, dual BTL, I2S in | Channel A bass, channel B TEAX25. PVDD is the 20 V PD rail |
| Network | Wi-Fi of the kit | Multi-room is Snapcast |
| Body | Printed frame, this repo | `mechanical/rev-g/cad/` |

Withdrawn, on purpose: Ethernet, PoE, 60 GHz radar, XVF3800, the custom MAIN, Dayton DAEX32SQ-8, Visaton EX 60 S as the product motor.

## 2. Why these two motors

A single 4 Ω exciter on the kit amp is cheaper and was a wrong shortcut. The TEAX25 is the catalog wideband unit that fits in 19 mm without the 130 g magnet of a DAEX32. The bass unit has to be a thin woofer exciter, 16 mm or less, because a midrange exciter does not make low bass on this panel. They need different signals, so the amplifier is a TAS5825M, not the mono TAS2780.

Depth check, using the deeper motor:

| Layer | mm |
| --- | ---: |
| Cloth | 1.5 |
| Panel | 8.0 |
| TEAX25 behind the panel | 19.0 |
| Air and rear skin | 1.5 |
| **Total** | **30.0** |

The woofer exciter is shallower, so it fits in the same cavity. Boards stay in the bottom bay. The HAT is 10 mm and does not stack on the motors.

Before the amp board is drawn, confirm that I2S (BCLK, LRCK, DOUT) is on the Satellite1 40-pin header or FX23L. The TAS5825M has no analog input.

Honest bar until a measurement: wide coverage, target around **50–60 Hz** after DSP, not a finished ±3 dB claim.

## 3. Frame

Printed in PETG or ASA, front face on the bed. Files: `mechanical/rev-g/cad/AP-G-FRAME.stl` and `.step`.

- Outer 320 × 400 × 30 mm.
- Panel drops in behind the front lip. Cloth wraps the lip.
- Bottom bay holds the HAT (88 × 88 × 10) and the Core (66 × 31 × 10), side by side.
- USB-C enters through the bottom edge.
- LD2450 looks into the room through a plastic window in the bay. No metal over the antenna.
- Four microphone slots in the bay lip. Align them to the HAT once the board is in hand.
- French cleat on the rear. Design load 3 kg × 3. The kit, exciter, and frame are well under the old 5 kg cone budget.

## 5. Bring-up

1. Print the frame. Confirm the HAT, Core, and USB plug sit in the bay without standing proud of 30 mm.
2. Bond the woofer exciter low and off-centre, and the TEAX25 higher and off-centre. Do not stack them.
3. Drive them from the TAS5825M, one channel each. Add cloth. Listen for buzz against the lip.
4. Fit the LD2450, point it into the room, tune zones in the Sat1 UI.
5. If the bass is short, change the panel or the woofer exciter. Do not parallel the two motors on one channel.

## 6. Multi-room

Two pictures are two Satellite1 units in one Snapcast group. Stereo is left and right assignment in Music Assistant, not a custom clock. That is the fast path, and it is already shipping in the kit firmware.
